Sinho Chewi's Website

I am an Assistant Professor of Statistics and Data Science at Yale University. I work at the interface of optimal transport, optimization, and probability, with a particular focus on the theory of sampling. My work has been recognized with an ALT 2023 Best Student Paper Award and an ICML 2026 Outstanding Paper Award.

Past Visits

I received my B.S. in Engineering Mathematics and Statistics from the University of California, Berkeley in 2018, and my PhD in Mathematics and Statistics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2023, advised by Philippe Rigollet. In Fall 2021, I participated in the Simons Institute program on Geometric Methods in Optimization and Sampling and co-organized (with Kevin Tian) a working group on the complexity of sampling. In Spring 2022, I visited Jonathan Niles-Weed at New York University. In Summer 2022, I was a research intern at Microsoft Research, supervised by Sébastien Bubeck and Adil Salim. In Fall 2023 and Spring 2024, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute for Advanced Study.

Research

I am broadly interested in the mathematics of machine learning and statistics. My work focuses on the complexity of log-concave sampling (see my book draft above), applications of optimal transport to probabilistic inference problems such as variational inference (Lambert et al. (2023); Diao et al. (2023); Jiang, Chewi, and Pooladian (2024)), and theory for diffusion generative models (Chen et al. (2023); Chewi et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2026); Kadkhodaie et al. (2026)).
